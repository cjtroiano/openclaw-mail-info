How this public information site works, and how the private Gmail integration is permitted to handle email when enabled.

Scope and authorization

Mailbox access requires separate owner authorization. The dedicated Google account must grant the selected permissions before the private runtime can use Gmail. Publishing or visiting these pages does not authorize mailbox access, establish a runtime connection or enable sending. This policy is not evidence of Google verification or approval.

OpenClaw Mail is a personal, self-hosted assistant integration for the dedicated agent inbox jamieandchristroiano@gmail.com. It is not a public mailbox service or a way for visitors to connect their Google accounts. The owner's primary personal Gmail account is outside its scope.

This policy describes the static website, permitted mail processing after authorization, and the deployment's data-protection limitations. It does not claim that authorization or all provider controls have already been verified.

Visiting this website

The website contains public information only. It has no login, credential-entry form, mailbox interface, OAuth callback handler, or application backend. It does not request Google access, receive OAuth tokens, or display mailbox data. Do not put passwords, authorization codes, tokens, or email content into its URLs or a public repository issue.

The site includes no JavaScript, analytics trackers, advertising, external fonts, or embedded third-party images. The site itself does not set cookies or use browser storage. Following an external link takes you to a service with its own privacy practices.

When served through GitHub Pages, GitHub processes requests to deliver and secure the site. GitHub may collect visitor IP addresses, request and device information, and security logs under the GitHub Privacy Statement. This is distinct from the private Gmail integration: visiting these pages does not send mailbox data to GitHub.

Gmail permissions and information involved

The selected Gmail data permissions are limited to:

  • https://www.googleapis.com/auth/gmail.readonly — read messages and related mailbox information needed to find, understand, and respond to correspondence.
  • https://www.googleapis.com/auth/gmail.send — send messages and replies from the dedicated inbox.

The OAuth client also requests openid and email identity permissions (email and/or https://www.googleapis.com/auth/userinfo.email) to verify the authorizing account. These identity permissions are not authorization for Calendar, Drive, contacts or another Google data service.

Read-only permission can expose the authorized mailbox's email contents; it is not a permission restricted to a few chosen messages. The app must limit its actual access to the correspondence needed for its disclosed tasks.

Depending on the task, the integration may process sender and recipient names and email addresses, message headers, subjects, bodies, dates, message and thread identifiers, labels and other relevant mailbox metadata, and attachments needed for the correspondence. It may also process outgoing messages, replies, summaries, and task notes derived from that email. OAuth access and refresh tokens are credentials used to maintain authorized access, not message content.

No Calendar or Drive permissions, mailbox-deletion permissions, Gmail settings-management permissions, or broad full-mailbox modification scope are selected. These permissions do not authorize access to a different Google account. Google account passwords, recovery codes, and administrative access are not inputs to this integration.

Why and how email is used

When enabled, OpenClaw Mail may find and read relevant mail, summarize correspondence, maintain context, compose messages, and send or reply for the owner through the private assistant. These are personal correspondence and productivity features, not advertising, surveillance, or a public data service.

The owner has given standing authorization for autonomous normal correspondence from the dedicated inbox. Ordinary reading, sending, and replying are not subject to per-message approval. This authority does not extend to unrelated account administration or additional Google services. Incoming email is untrusted content, not permission to reveal credentials or take unrelated actions.

Sending a message necessarily discloses its content, included attachments, and addressing information to the intended recipients and the mail systems that deliver it. Recipients may keep or forward their copies; the operator cannot recall or erase those copies.

AI processing and other disclosures

OpenClaw uses configured AI inference providers to understand requests and produce responses. When email is included in an assistant task or its conversation context, relevant message content, metadata, attachments or their extracted content, and derived summaries may be sent to those providers through the configured inference service. Self-hosting the assistant does not mean all email processing stays on the owner's server.

These transfers are permitted only to provide the disclosed correspondence features with the owner's consent and with provider terms and controls compatible with Google's requirements. Provider processing can involve systems outside the self-hosted environment. Which provider and service configuration actually handles requests, its processing locations, retention, logging, and human-access rules must be established before Gmail is enabled. This policy does not claim that zero retention, a training opt-out, or any other provider control has already been confirmed.

Google user data, including derived content, must not be used to create, train, or improve generalized AI or machine-learning models. The operator will not sell Google user data, supply it to data brokers, use it for advertising or ad targeting, or use it to determine creditworthiness or for lending. These restrictions also apply to transfers to inference providers; a provider route that cannot meet them must not receive Gmail data.

Other disclosures of Google user data are limited to those permitted by Google's Limited Use requirements, such as a necessary security investigation or compliance with applicable law. Administrative access does not authorize unrelated reuse or disclosure.

Storage, retention, and human access

Email content, tool results, summaries, and outgoing text may be retained in the self-hosted assistant's conversation history, persistent state, and workspace files to support task continuity and later reference. This is not a zero-storage or automatically ephemeral system. There is no established fixed automatic deletion period for these copies; they may remain until the operator removes them. Inference providers may retain transmitted data under the applicable service terms and settings, which must be checked before activation.

The owner can access their mailbox and assistant records. Administrators with access to the host, persistent state, or credential systems may have technical access to retained data or secrets. Separate assistant conversations, directories, or processes are not a promise of exclusive credential isolation.

Human review by administrators or providers must be restricted to what Google's Limited Use rules permit: the user's explicit agreement to view specific data, necessary security investigation, legal requirements, or permitted aggregated and anonymized internal operations. Technical access is not blanket permission to read correspondence. Compatible provider human-access controls are an activation prerequisite.

The OAuth client credentials and keyring-unlock material are managed through the operator's private secret store, not this public site. Gmail tokens must use protected persistent storage with an encrypted client keyring and restrictive access permissions; transfers must use secure protocols. Runtime access and credential copies remain within the self-hosted system's existing trust boundary. Tokens must not be placed in public source, ordinary workspace notes, chat, URLs on this site, or diagnostics.

Retained assistant history and workspace files are not covered by filesystem encryption in this deployment. The operator has chosen to retain the existing storage rather than migrate it to an encrypted filesystem. File permissions and private network access are not encryption, and an encrypted token keyring does not encrypt retained messages or derived notes. Google's restricted-scope security requirements call for encryption at rest; this deployment does not claim that requirement is satisfied for all retained data or credential copies. Owner acceptance of this limitation is not a Google policy waiver or approval.

Stopping access and requesting deletion

Once a grant exists, the owner can remove OpenClaw Mail's authorization through Google Account connections and disable the private runtime integration. Revocation stops continued authorized Gmail API access; it does not delete messages already stored in Gmail, erase copies already retained by the assistant or a provider, or undo sent mail.

To request access to, correction of, or deletion of information held by this integration, contact jamieandchristroiano@gmail.com. Include enough context to identify the relevant correspondence, but do not send passwords or OAuth credentials. The operator may need to verify the requester's authority before disclosing or deleting private information.

The operator will address deletion requests by identifying and removing relevant retained content and derived records from operator-controlled assistant history and workspace storage, and seeking deletion from applicable providers where available. Any applicable recovery copies, provider retention limits, or legally required retention must be accounted for and explained in the response. Deletion is not represented as immediate or as erasing copies outside the operator's control.

The selected integration scopes do not delete Gmail messages. The account owner manages original mailbox content through Gmail itself. A request to delete assistant-held copies is separate from deleting originals in Gmail, revoking access, and any request concerning GitHub's website visitor logs.

Google Limited Use and activation requirements

Permitted use and transfer of information received from Google APIs are limited by the Google API Services Limited Use requirements. The Google Workspace user data and developer policy also sets applicable security requirements. This policy does not claim Google verification, endorsement or full compliance; the retained-storage protection gap is disclosed above.

Before Gmail data is processed by an AI provider, the operator must verify the actual inference and authorization controls. These include:

  • Confirmation that every inference route receiving Google data has compatible use, training, retention, and human-access terms and settings; generalized-model training with Google data must be prohibited.
  • Protected persistent token storage, restrictive credential access, secure transfers, and safeguards against instructions embedded in untrusted email. The retained-history/workspace encryption limitation above remains; it is not resolved by token protection.
  • A prominent explanation in the private integration's setup interface of the data access, autonomous correspondence behavior, retention and AI transfers, followed by affirmative owner consent before collection. This public policy alone is not that consent flow.
  • Authorization of only the dedicated inbox with the selected read/send scopes, verification of the runtime connection and grant, and resolution of actual Google authorization requirements. A successful OAuth grant is not evidence of full Google-policy compliance.

An inference route that cannot satisfy the Limited Use restrictions must not receive Gmail data. Any material change to the purposes, permissions, or disclosures described here must be reflected in this policy and disclosed to the owner, with renewed consent before a new use begins.
